# Stage editor

The Stages tab is the Python app's `stage_builder.py` rebuilt as a hand-drawn infinite canvas in `src/ui/stages`.

It never computes layout or prompt text itself: positions come from the core `layout()` and every edit goes through the core `Workflow` edit API, so the compiled prompt matches the Python app.

## Controller

One hook owns the editor so the canvas, the inspector, the menus and the action registry drive the same state.

[[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#useStages]] holds the focus and ticked selection, the viewport, the measured scene, the floating menu and the saved-workflow baseline. Workflow edits go back to the app store through `onChange`, so undo/redo, autosave and the prompt panel treat them like any other edit; typing in one field merges into one undo step. Focus and selection that an undo removes are pruned.

## Edits

[[src/ui/stages/edits.ts]] maps each StageBuilder callback to a pure `WorkflowDict -> WorkflowDict` function.

It covers insert at a gap, into a branch or after the focus (a new Intentions block starts from the Task tab's settings), remove, move, drag-drop onto a gap or parallel frame, loop and parallel from the selection with the Python app's check messages as the hint, every inspector field, `_num` rules for loop numbers (target stored as a Python float), uses-panel blank filling through `set_use`, and the on-cancel stages.

## Canvas

The scene is the core layout measured with the canvas fonts and drawn as rough.js SVG paths. No canvas font is under 12px at 100% (tags, the on-cancel pill and prompt or function bodies included), the same floor as the chrome.

- [[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#buildScene]] lays the workflow out at height 0 (the canvas is infinite); text is measured with an OffscreenCanvas, re-measured once web fonts load.
- [[src/ui/stages/SceneView.tsx#SceneView]] draws loop and parallel frames, the function shelf and links, edges, cards (tags, a "Group" pill, ×, uses footer, read-only hatch; the Group pill (a tick box with the word "Group" in a hairline pill) and × sit together at the top right and show only on hover, on the focused card, on a ticked card, and, for Group pills, on every card while any is ticked, so a resting card is just its label, name and description; hidden controls take no clicks; each is a 24px-tall target with a soft fill on hover, drawn in the muted ink rather than the pale edge grey, and carries a tooltip: "Tick to group with other blocks: Loop (L) or Parallel (G)" and "Delete stage" or the block's own noun; there is no grip, since a press anywhere on the card drags it; an empty Goal shows an accent "✎ Describe the goal" pill in place of its body line, the flow's first call to action), gap buttons, branch tiles and loop return arrows. Shapes come from [[src/ui/stages/sketch.ts]] with seeds hashed from element ids, cached by their arguments.
- Content uses fixed light colours; dark mode inverts the SVG layer with `--canvas-filter`, except that the cancel lane's fill and focus halo (`.sk-cancel-fill`, `.sk-cancel-halo`) take dark-only light values chosen so the inversion lands on a near-neutral fill and a quiet halo rather than a muddy maroon and salmon glow, and the dot grid moves and scales with the view.
- [[src/ui/stages/StageCanvas.tsx#StageCanvas]] pans on empty-space drag, Space+drag, middle button and wheel (up and down like the Task board; Shift+wheel sideways), zooms toward the cursor on Ctrl/Cmd+wheel (about 10% a notch; the zoom island's − / + / % and Ctrl/Cmd +, −, 0 instead glide there around the free area's centre over [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#ZOOM_GLIDE_MS]], each press stepping from where a running glide is heading) or around the fingers on a two-finger touch pinch ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#pinchView]]; on phones the pinch is the zoom: there is no zoom island there and Fit sits in the stage bar), focuses on click (focus is the accent outline and the inspector, never a tick: a click, an add or keyboard travel leaves the ticks alone), ticks with Shift / Ctrl / Cmd + click or the tick boxes (only ticks feed Loop and Parallel; a right-click on an unticked card offers to loop just that card), and drags cards (a solid ghost on a lifted two-layer shadow, so the cards it passes over never show through it, that keeps the grab offset while the card fades in place and stays, with the line under it, a little inside the free canvas rather than slide under a panel or off the screen's edge, every gap marked faintly and the one it would land on in bold, a screen-sized line under the ghost saying what a release does from the controller's `dropEffect` — move it here, drop on a gap to reorder, or why it is refused — and auto-pan near the free area's edges) onto the nearest gap or a parallel frame; when that gap is one of the card's own (a no-op) but the card has travelled at least [[src/ui/stages/StageCanvas.tsx#SNAP_SHARE]] of its width, it snaps to the nearest gap ahead that does move it, so a body drag released over the next card still reorders. A release that moves nothing glides the ghost back onto its card instead of vanishing. Enter or a double-click on a card puts the caret in the inspector's first field. From the keyboard, arrows walk the cards in reading order and Home / End jump to the ends ([[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#stepCard]]); the card is focused as by a click and [[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#cardAnnouncement]] is read out through a polite live region, so the inspector, Delete and Alt+arrow moves work without a mouse. The viewport focus ring shows for keyboard travel only (arrows, Tab), not after a click or an editing key such as `S`.
- Card type is sized for reading at the opening zoom: 12px bold eyebrows, 16px Kalam names, 13–14px bodies ([[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#FONTS]]).
- Empty cards say how to fill them in: an empty goal reads [[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#GOAL_EMPTY]], and the empty on-cancel lane says it is optional ([[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#ONCANCEL_EMPTY]]) and carries a `+ Add stage` pill (hit role `addcancel`, set in the UI face like the eyebrow beside it) that adds the first cancel stage in one click; empty-card copy is line-balanced so it never ends on a one-word orphan. With no blocks yet the one + after the Goal is the next step: larger and in the accent, titled "Add the first stage". With nothing ticked, the status line is one short how-to line ([[src/ui/stages/edits.ts#IDLE_HINT]]) that shows only while the flow has at most [[src/ui/components/StagesMount.tsx#HINT_BLOCKS_MAX]] block and fades out from the second on (the gestures stay listed under Help); a status that says something, such as what is ticked, always shows. This copy is canvas-only ([[src/ui/stages/scene.ts#cardLines]] draws it in the lines the core layout sized for the Python app's wording) and never reaches the prompt.
- Pan and zoom from the wheel, pinch, drag and edge auto-pan never jump: the controller's `setView` moves where the camera is heading and the shown view chases it each frame ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#chaseView]], an exponential ease with time constant [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#CHASE_MS]], frame-rate independent), around the point the move keeps still ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#fixedPoint]]) so a wheel zoom stays anchored on the cursor every frame; later input aims from that heading, so quick wheel notches add up. Keyboard travel glides over [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#ZOOM_GLIDE_MS]]. Where [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#motionOk]] is false (reduced motion, jsdom) the view jumps straight there.
- [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts]] has the pan/zoom math: 10–300%, a home view at a readable zoom that centres a short flow at the optical centre ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#opticalCenterY]], a little above the middle) at 100% (never enlarged past [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#HOME_ZOOM_MAX]], so an empty flow opens at the same zoom as a populated one) and starts a long one at the goal, and fit-to-content that avoids the open islands (top bars, side panels, a phone bottom sheet and the bottom zoom row, via `data-inset` in [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#visibleArea]]). Fit ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#fitFlow]]) never zooms out past [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#FIT_MIN]] (80%, where a card's eyebrow and name still read at about 10px and 13px on screen; a home view never opens below it either): a flow too long for that pans instead of shrinking, shown at 80% around the focused card and its neighbours ([[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#fitAnchor]]); when the three don't fit at 80% (a narrow strip between the inspector and the Prompt panel) it eases out further, never below [[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#FOCUS_MIN]] (70%), until they do, and only past that frames the card alone, so the focused card is never the one cut off, or from the Goal with nothing focused, without empty board past either end. Whenever flow cards lie beyond the free area's left or right edge (a long flow, or one running under the Prompt panel), [[src/ui/stages/StageCanvas.tsx#StageCanvas]] shows a chip at that edge just under the flow's row, "N more stages →" ("blocks" when not all are stages) or "← Goal" when only the start is off the left, and a click glides the board about 80% of the free width that way ([[src/ui/stages/viewport.ts#pageAlong]]), stopping with the flow's end inside the edge. Until the next manual pan or zoom the view counts as fitted, and a panel opening or closing or the flow changing size fits again for the new free area (animated), so closing the Prompt panel re-centres the flow and opening the inspector keeps the focused card's neighbours beside it. Even when the view was not fitted, a panel opening beside a focused card whose neighbours it leaves cut off refits the same way (the card with both neighbours whole, down to 70%), so the strip between two panels never shows a card and a half. The controller fits on first open, saves the view only once it has been shown, and re-homes when the tab opens on a remembered view that shows no flow card whole. The free area left by the panels is the real viewport: when a panel opens, closes or is resized, [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#reframe]] glides the view the least amount that keeps what showed before (the whole flow, else the cards that showed whole) inside the new free area, and an enlarged opening view eases back toward 100% once the flow outgrows it. When a panel opens or closes or a block is added or removed and the whole flow showed before, [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#frameFlow]] keeps it whole and centres it in the free area ([[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#centerOn]], at the optical centre) with [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#FRAME_PAD]] clear on every side, trailing + and on-cancel lane included, so it never crowds one panel's edge with the other side empty; when it no longer fits at this zoom it eases out just enough, never below [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#AUTO_ZOOM_MIN]] (below that card type is too small to edit by), and a zoom eased out that way springs back toward 100% once there is room again. A block added or removed also re-shows the flow whole and centred when it fits at that floor, even if it had been panned part way, so a short flow never stays half off screen behind a chip. A flow that did not show whole, or no longer fits even at that floor, is shown whole if it fits at the current zoom (the least pan); otherwise the block just added or focused comes into view first and the Goal joins it when both fit, and with nothing focused (the inspector closed) the Goal comes back when it showed before, when the inspector has just folded away on the right, or when it is at most [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#GOAL_PULL]] of the free width away, so a short flow reads from its start. A panel closing (more room) also shows the whole flow again when it now fits at 80% or more, as adding a block does. Focusing or adding a block pans only when it is off the free area, by the smallest delta, and what it brings into view is [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#revealBox]]: the card with the gap button (+) just after it, so a last card never leaves its + on a panel's edge; a long flow that runs on under the Prompt panel fades into its inner edge (a short gradient from the canvas colour) so the next card reads as "more this way", and the drawing is clipped at that edge so no sliver of a card shows again in the strip between the panel and the screen edge; selecting a visible card or dropping one never moves the board, and a drop leaves the selection as it was.

## Prompt flags UI

The Stages tab shows a workflow's A/B variants and lets the user choose between them; the logic is [[core#Prompt flags]].

- **Forks on the canvas**: [[src/ui/stages/SceneView.tsx#SceneView]] draws each fork ([[src/core/forks.ts#forkGroups]]) as an orange dashed frame with an `A/B · flag` pill on top and, above each lane's card, a tag with the variant letter and condition. The variant the flag values choose is a solid orange tag with a tick; the other is an outlined grey tag saying `not selected` (`removed` in strip mode), and its card is dimmed (fainter in strip mode, with a dashed stroke through it). A lone `when` block, with no fork around it, wears the same tag on its top edge. Hover or focus brings a dimmed card back to near full.
- **Tags are controls**: every A/B tag is drawn by [[src/ui/stages/TagsLayer.tsx#TagsLayer]], a second SVG over the canvas, from [[src/ui/stages/tags.ts#sceneTags]]. Each is a focusable button with a name, a tooltip (`Click to switch to variant A`) and a hover state. Pressing a lane's or a lone block's variant tag, or the lane's empty room, switches the flag values to that variant ([[src/core/forks.ts#variantChoice]]: a bool flag flips, a text flag takes the value its `==` names); where no single value does that, or on a fork's `A/B · flag` pill, the Flags popover opens beside the press with that flag's control focused. Enter or Space on a focused tag presses it.
- **Inline branches on cards**: a stage, prompt or function whose text has `{{#if}}` branches wears an `A/B · play_bot · 2 branches → B` tag on its top edge (right-aligned beside a `when` tag): the flags, the number of branches, and the letter every chain follows (`→ mixed` or `→ none` when they differ or none applies). It follows the Flags values and mode live, and pressing it opens the Flags popover on that flag.
- **Flags tool**: once the workflow declares a flag the stage bar gains a Flags tool ([[src/ui/stages/FlagsPopover.tsx#FlagsPopover]], palette command "Prompt flags") that opens a popover above the bar (full width on phones): a checkbox per bool flag, a text field per text flag, and an Embed both | Strip the others choice. Values and mode live in the app config, so they persist, undo and reach the Prompt panel and Copy as they change; Esc or a press outside closes it. A palette command swaps the mode. Opened from a tag it floats beside the press instead (on the side with room, inside the window) and puts the focus on that flag; opened from the stage bar or the palette it stays above the bar.
- **Inspector**: the Goal form's Prompt flags section declares flags (name, On / off or Text, default, label; `+ Add flag`, Remove) and lists [[src/core/workflow.ts#Workflow]]'s flag problems. Every stage, prompt and function form has a Show when field: only a condition that reads and names a declared flag is written, otherwise the reason shows under it. A block with `{{#if}}` text also gets a read-only A/B branches panel: each chain's branches as rows (`Variant A · play_bot`, `Variant B · otherwise`) with the first 160 characters of each, the chosen one marked and the others saying whether they stay in the prompt. In the prompt fields the `{{#if}}`, `{{#elif}}`, `{{#else}}`, `{{/if}}` and `{{@flag}}` markers show as orange chips like function calls calls.

## Dictation

Every prompt field, and its expand dialog, has a mic that types what is said into the text using the browser's own speech recognition.

[[src/ui/lib/useDictation.ts#useDictation]] runs the Web Speech API (`SpeechRecognition`, or Chrome's and Safari's `webkitSpeechRecognition`) continuously in the page's language; promptdraw itself sends nothing anywhere, though the browser may use its vendor's speech service. Each finished phrase goes in at the text area's caret (the end in Preview) through the field's own `onChange`, so it merges into the field's undo step and reaches the prompt like typing; [[src/ui/lib/useDictation.ts#insertPhrase]] trims it and spaces it from its neighbours. While listening the mic turns red and pulses (steady under reduced motion) and a line under the field shows the phrase still being heard, or why it stopped (microphone blocked, none found). Pressing the mic again, opening the expand dialog or closing the panel stops it. Where the browser has no speech recognition (Firefox) the mic is disabled with a tooltip saying so.

## Workflow library

[[src/ui/stages/library.ts]] stands in for the Python app's `workflows/` folder: `to_yaml` text keyed by slug in `promptdraw:workflows`, newest first.

Loading a workflow (a library entry, New workflow or a YAML file) opens on the whole flow so its shape reads, holds for [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#INTRO_HOLD_MS]], then glides over [[src/ui/stages/useStages.ts#INTRO_GLIDE_MS]] into the home view; it skips straight there when the whole flow already shows at about that zoom or motion is reduced, and a pan or zoom during the hold keeps the user's view.

Save (`Ctrl/Cmd+S` or File ▾) asks before overwriting an entry that changed since it was loaded; New, Load and opening a YAML file ask before discarding unsaved changes. Dirty means the current `to_yaml` differs from the baseline: the saved library entry, else the workflow as New or an opened file brought it in, else the untouched default, so a fresh workflow shows no badge. A workflow that was never saved and is unchanged shows no status at all. The viewport is kept in `promptdraw:stageView`.

### Starter workflows

File ▾ → Templates opens a built-in workflow from [[src/ui/stages/starters.ts#STARTERS]] through the discard guard, unsaved, as its own baseline; Save puts it in the library like any other.

While nothing is open (the board holds the empty default workflow) and the right edge is free, a Demo workflows island there ([[src/ui/stages/DemoPanel.tsx#DemoPanel]]) lists the same starters as cards (name, what it is for, stage and loop counts) and opens one in a click; once a workflow is open (a demo, a file, an edit) it steps aside. The canvas pads itself clear of it; below 900px it steps aside.

Each is meant to run as written and to show what a workflow can hold. **Sprint Chaser** pulls a backlog, plans by value ÷ points, then loops pick → build ∥ tests → verify on a `burn_down` reward (≥ 90, 12 passes) with a per-ticket `criteria_met` loop (100, 3 passes) nested inside; `Ticket check` and `Ship gate` are function blocks, and on cancel it parks the ticket on a `wip/` branch. **Council of QC UI** captures a screen across a device matrix (small, notched and tall phones in both orientations with safe-area insets, a foldable, a tablet, laptop and ultrawide, light and dark, plus the keyboard open), then loops five read-only reviewers in parallel (each a `Verdict` call with its own lens, one of them Device QA for safe areas, aspect ratios and orientation), a chair and a fix stage on a `council_score` reward: the lowest reviewer score minus one per open blocker, ≥ 8 within 4 passes. **The Holy Audit of Engineer** is language-agnostic: it maps the codebase and its git history, runs the project's own instruments (build, tests twice for flakiness, linters, dependency, secret and licence scans, churn × complexity), then eight read-only lenses run in parallel, each an `Audit lens` call: architecture, code health, testing, security (OWASP Top 10:2025), reliability, performance, delivery (SLSA provenance, the five DORA metrics) and data/APIs/docs. Every finding needs evidence, a severity and a fix card. A chair ranks them by risk ÷ effort, then a `Fix card` → re-audit loop runs on a `holiness_score` reward (≥ 85 within 10 passes) and the run ends with guardrails against each fixed class of flaw. Stages that write a report under the repo are not read-only, since the read-only note forbids creating project files. Each pins only atomic commits, so branch and worktree stay at the app's setting (off by default). [[src/ui/stages/starters.ts#starterWorkflow]] returns a cleaned copy.

## Tests

Unit tests cover the edit helpers, viewport math and scene lookups; component tests drive the editor through the app.

`edits.test.ts` checks each mutation against the core and the prompt; `viewport.test.ts` covers pan, zoom, the camera chase (cursor stays anchored, frame-rate independent, lands exactly), fit (and Fit's readable floor), drop targets and frameFlow; `reframe.test.ts` covers keeping cards in view when a panel changes the free area and the reveal box taking in a card's trailing +; `StageEditor.test.tsx` covers drawing, adding and editing, the prompt field's Markdown marks, Preview and large editor, undo, selection and loop, drag-drop, menus, wheel zoom, and library save/new/load with the discard guard.

### Inspector docks right and folds away

Focusing the Goal opens a right-docked inspector with a Write / Preview field and hides the Prompt panel; a zoom-row press keeps it, a toolbar press folds it away.

The Prompt panel stays mounted while hidden and shows again once the inspector folds away.

### Escape leaves a field then closes the inspector

Escape in a field of the stage inspector leaves the field; a second Escape closes the inspector, so the keyboard alone gets back to the canvas and the Prompt panel.

### Inspector width and name row

Until resized the inspector opens at [[src/ui/stages/dock.ts#DOCK_SHARE]] of the window (floored at the dock minimum); a stage's Model and Read-only sit in the name row and still drive the prompt.

### Dictation into a prompt

With a stubbed recognizer, the mic in a stage's prompt toggles listening, shows the interim phrase, and puts a finished phrase in at the caret so the prompt follows.

### Dictated phrase spacing
